# OP-029 — Child-to-parent decisions and structured interviews

**Status:** Proposed · **Priority:** P1 · **Effort:** M–L · **Origin:** pi-subagents 0.83.0
**Dependencies:** OP-026, OP-028; surfaces through OP-014.

## Problem and outcome
A child encountering missing requirements must not guess, silently expand scope, or wait invisibly. Users need a durable question/answer path that preserves the identity of the requesting worker and the decision it needs.

## Scope
Implement three structured message types: blocking decision request, structured interview, and non-blocking progress update. Persist request ID, parent/child/task identity, question, bounded evidence, options, blocking scope, and lifecycle. Route a question to the immediate parent; let that parent escalate a distinct linked request to the owner. Present pending decisions in the attention inbox with the affected child and worktree. Resume only the blocked step after a correlated answer.

Differentiate request delivery, answer acceptance, child acknowledgement, expiry, withdrawal, and cancellation. Support independent work continuing while one child awaits input. A parent cannot synchronously block on a child while leaving no route for that child's decision; detect and reject such deadlock-prone orchestration.

## Safety and recovery
A product decision or supervisor message is not an exact-action approval. Mutations still require the policy gate. Replies are scoped to the exact authorized lineage; a reply to a parent does not automatically answer a grandchild's request. Duplicate or late replies cannot unlock a newer request. Progress messages should not repeatedly wake the model or expose secrets.

## Acceptance criteria
- Concurrent child questions receive independent IDs and the right answers.
- Parent/child restart preserves pending questions and prevents duplicate continuation.
- Foreign-session, expired, withdrawn, and already-resolved replies are rejected or idempotently reported.
- Cancelling a task closes dependent questions and prevents late resume.
- Structured interviews retain owner choices as evidence without converting them into tool permission.
